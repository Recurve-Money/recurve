Protocol

Vaults

Every fund is an ERC-4626 vault with voting shares. It holds the idle balance, counts what is deployed, and lets capital out to a strategy only through its governor.

Standard where it counts

RecurveVault is ERC-4626, so deposit, mint, convertToAssets, previewRedeem and the rest behave the way any integrator expects. It also extends ERC20Votes, which is what lets the governor snapshot voting weight, and ERC20Permit, so shares can be approved by signature. Shares carry the asset's decimals (no decimals offset) and an empty vault mints 1:1.

Deposit cap

depositCap is a ceiling on totalAssets(), set in the constructor and enforced through the standard hooks: maxDeposit returns the room left under the cap in asset terms (zero once totalAssets() reaches it, whoever the receiver is) and maxMint the same room in shares. deposit and mint revert above those with OpenZeppelin's ERC4626ExceededMaxDeposit and its mint counterpart. Because the cap is on total assets, profit a strategy brings back consumes cap room.

raiseDepositCap(uint256 newCap)   // deployer only, emits DepositCapRaised(previousCap, newCap)

One-directional: whoever deployed the vault can raise it, anyone else reverts with OnlyDeployer, and a value at or below the current cap reverts with CapCannotDecrease. The Recurve Fund starts at 10,000 USDG (DEPOSIT_CAP in the deploy settings).

Accounting

totalAssets() = idle balance + deployedAssets
float()       = idle balance

deployedAssets is carried explicitly rather than inferred, because a position mid-strategy is neither in the balance nor visible to balanceOf. It is one number for the whole vault, not one per proposal: every fundStrategy adds to it and one settleStrategy zeroes it.

Between return and settle

When a strategy sends assets back to the vault, the balance rises while deployedAssets is still counted, so totalAssets() and the share price are inflated until settle runs. Claims and instant redemptions in that window are priced on the inflated number. The runner settles in the round after the return executes to keep the window short.

How capital moves

fundStrategy(target, assets) is the governor's way out: it transfers assets to the target, adds them to deployedAssets, and reverts with FloatTooLow if the request exceeds the idle balance. Only the governor can call it, and the governor only calls it from an executed proposal. settleStrategy(returned) is the way back on paper: it zeroes deployedAssets and emits StrategySettled(returned, pnl). It does not move tokens and does not check that returned arrived — the strategy transfers assets back on its own, and the governor reports the number.

Depositors have a second exit the queue does not cover. The standard ERC-4626 withdraw and redeem are not overridden: they pay from the idle balance and, when it is short, revert inside the asset's transfer instead of queueing. maxWithdraw and maxRedeem report the holder's full position, not what the balance can pay. Use requestRedeem, as the app and SDK do, to get the queue.

Redemption

requestRedeem(uint256 shares, address receiver, address owner) -> uint256 requestId
claimRedeem(uint256 requestId) -> uint256 assets

requestRedeem pays instantly when previewRedeem(shares) fits in the idle balance and returns type(uint256).max as a sentinel. Otherwise it moves the shares into the vault's own custody, appends to the queue, emits WithdrawalQueued(owner, requestId, shares), and returns the queue index. The receiver is fixed at request time.

claimRedeem prices at claim time, not request time — that is the whole reason the queue exists. It reverts with RequestNotReady while the idle balance cannot cover the shares' current value, RequestAlreadyClaimed on a repeat, and NotRequestOwner unless the caller is the address that owned the shares when the request was made (not the receiver, not an operator). Ready requests can be claimed in any order; there is no expiry and no cancel.

Note

Escrowed shares keep counting toward supply until the claim burns them, which is why totalAssets does not net them out — and they carry no voting weight while they wait, because the vault delegates to nobody.

Votes

Voting weight is checkpointed by block. getPastVotes(holder, block) is what the governor reads at a proposal's snapshot; it is zero for a holder who never called delegate. getPastTotalSupply(block) is the denominator for the veto threshold and counts every share, delegated or not. A delegation in the same block as a proposal is not visible to that proposal's snapshot.
